🗼 Paris, France - Eiffel Tower

Overview: The Eiffel Tower is an iconic iron lattice tower located on the Champ de Mars in Paris. Built in 1889 for the World's Fair, it has become the most recognizable symbol of Paris and one of the most visited monuments in the world.

History: Designed by Gustave Eiffel, the tower was initially intended to be temporary but has remained a permanent fixture. It stands 330 meters (1,083 feet) tall and was the world's tallest man-made structure until 1930.

Visitor Experience: Visitors can take elevators or stairs to three levels, each offering spectacular views of Paris. The tower is beautifully illuminated at night and attracts millions of visitors annually.

🏛️ Rome, Italy - Colosseum

Overview: The Colosseum, also known as the Flavian Amphitheatre, is an ancient amphitheater in Rome, Italy. It is one of the most impressive works of Roman architecture and engineering, and one of the most recognizable structures of ancient Rome.

History: Construction began under Emperor Vespasian in 72 AD and was completed under his son Titus in 80 AD. The amphitheater could hold approximately 50,000 spectators and was used for gladiatorial contests and public spectacles.

Visitor Experience: Today, the Colosseum stands as a testament to Roman engineering and is one of the most visited tourist attractions in Rome. Visitors can explore the ancient structure and imagine the grand events that took place within its walls.

🗽 New York, USA - Statue of Liberty

Overview: The Statue of Liberty is a colossal neoclassical sculpture located on Liberty Island in New York Harbor. It is a universal symbol of freedom and democracy, and one of the most iconic monuments in the United States.

History: A gift from France to the United States, the statue was designed by French sculptor Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi and dedicated in 1886. It was intended to commemorate the centennial of American independence and the alliance between France and the United States.

Visitor Experience: Visitors can take ferries to Liberty Island and explore the statue up close. Those who are fit can climb to the crown for panoramic views of New York Harbor and the Manhattan skyline. The statue has welcomed millions of immigrants to America.
